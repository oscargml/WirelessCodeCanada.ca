Bill Shock & Overage Charges — What You Actually Owe

"Bill shock" is the term for a wireless or internet bill that's far higher than you expected. The Wireless Code puts hard limits on the most common causes — here's how to push back.

The two caps that protect you

Charge typeMonthly capWhat it means
Domestic data overage$50Charges must stop at $50/cycle unless you expressly agree to continue.
International data roaming$100Roaming data charges must stop at $100/cycle without your express consent.

These caps are per account, per monthly cycle, and apply by default — your carrier cannot remove them silently.

Common bill-shock scenarios

1. "Unlimited" plan with overage charges

If your plan was sold as unlimited but you're billed for data overage, that's a direct contradiction of your Critical Information Summary. Dispute it.

2. Roaming you didn't authorize

Auto-connecting to a foreign network and racking up data without an opt-in notice violates the $100 cap rule.

3. Mid-contract price increase

A "plan update" that raises your monthly price without your express consent is generally not permitted on a fixed-term contract.

4. Charges after you cancelled

Being billed for service after a confirmed cancellation — or a cancellation fee post-Oct 2025 — is recoverable.

How to dispute it in 4 steps

  1. Pull your bill and your Critical Information Summary side by side.
  2. Identify the exact charge and the cap or term it breaches.
  3. Generate a complaint letter citing the right section.
  4. If ignored after 14 days, escalate to the CCTS.

Prevent the next one: set a data alert, turn off data roaming before travelling, and ask your carrier to confirm caps are active in writing.
