Your CRTC Internet Code Rights — Explained

The Internet Code applies to home internet services from Canada's largest providers. It mirrors many Wireless Code protections so you're not trapped by confusing contracts or surprise hikes.

1. Clear, upfront pricing

Your provider must give you a clear summary of your price, term, data allowance, and any introductory/regular pricing. If your "promo" jumped to a higher rate you weren't clearly told about, that's a problem.

2. No surprise price increases

Providers can't raise the price or change key terms of a fixed-term contract without your express consent. They must notify you clearly of changes.

3. Bill-management tools & overage clarity

You're entitled to clear information about data usage and any overage charges, so you can avoid bill shock on capped internet plans.

4. Easy cancellation

You can cancel at any time, and (post-2025) you cannot be charged a cancellation or switching fee for leaving. You only pay for service used up to the cancellation date and any valid equipment balance.

5. Security deposits & disconnection rules

The Code limits how deposits are handled and sets rules around disconnection, so you get fair notice before service is cut.

6. A trial period

New customers get a trial period to cancel a new internet contract at little or no cost if the service doesn't meet what was promised.
