Cancellation & Switching Fees Are Banned in Canada — Here's How to Get Your Money Back

The single biggest barrier to switching carriers is gone. Since June 12, 2026, the CRTC enforces a ban on the fees providers used to charge for leaving or changing a plan — on both cellphone and home internet service.

Bottom line: No activation fees, no plan-change fees, no cancellation or switching fees. Charged one anyway? It's recoverable — generate a refund letter in 2 minutes.

What changed, and when

Parliament amended the Telecommunications Act and the changes came into force on October 30, 2025. To give providers time to update their systems, the CRTC set the enforcement date at June 12, 2026 (Telecom Decision CRTC 2026-43, announced March 12, 2026). That enforcement date has now passed — the ban is live and being applied.

Which fees are now prohibited

The amended Act prohibits any fee whose main purpose is to discourage you from changing or cancelling service. In practice that means:

  • Activation / set-up fees for starting a new plan
  • Plan-change fees for moving between plans with the same provider
  • Cancellation and switching (early-exit) fees for leaving
  • "Administrative" exit fees dressed up under another name

The rules cover both wireless cellphone plans and home internet plans.

What providers can still charge

The ban is specific — a few charges remain legitimate:

Still allowedNo longer allowed
Remaining balance on a device financing agreement (the phone itself)Cancellation / switching fee
Usage charges up to your cancellation dateActivation or plan-change fee
In-home installation service"Administrative" exit fee
Optional add-ons you expressly agreed to buyAny fee meant to discourage leaving

Note: a device financing balance is not a penalty — it's the unpaid cost of your phone, and it's still valid. Ask for it in writing.

Were you charged one anyway? How to get a refund

  1. Contact your provider first and ask them to reverse the charge, referencing the ban. Get a ticket or reference number.
  2. If they refuse or stall, escalate — for free — to the CCTS, the independent telecom complaints body. It can order a provider to repay proven losses up to $5,000 per complaint.
  3. Put it in writing. A clear, cited demand letter gets faster results than a phone call — generate one here, free.

Now that leaving is free, should you switch?

The whole point of the ban is to make switching painless. If you've stayed on an overpriced plan just to avoid an exit fee, that reason is gone. Compare what's available before you renew — many Canadians save $30–$40 a month by moving.
