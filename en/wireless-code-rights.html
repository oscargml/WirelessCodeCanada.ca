Your CRTC Wireless Code Rights — Explained

The Wireless Code is a mandatory CRTC code of conduct that applies to all individual and small-business wireless contracts in Canada. These are your legal protections — here's what each one means and how to use it.

Updated for 2025: As of October 30, 2025, the amended Telecom Act bans all fees for switching providers or cancelling service.

1. The $50 monthly data overage cap

Your carrier must suspend data overage charges once they reach $50 in a single monthly billing cycle — and they cannot charge you more unless you expressly agree to keep going. This applies even if your plan is described as "pay-per-use".

2. The $100 international roaming cap

Data roaming charges are capped at $100 per monthly cycle without your express consent. If you returned from a trip to a four-figure roaming bill you never agreed to, that's a Wireless Code violation.

3. Free device unlocking

All phones must be sold unlocked. If you have an older locked device, your carrier must unlock it free of charge on request — no exceptions, no fees.

4. No cancellation fees after 24 months

On a typical term contract, any early-cancellation fee must be reduced to zero by the end of 24 months. Charging an early-cancellation fee after two years is not allowed.

5. No switching or cancellation fees (new, Oct 2025)

The Telecom Act amendments now prohibit carriers from charging any fee simply to cancel or switch providers. If you were charged one after October 30, 2025, you're entitled to a refund.

6. No surprise contract changes

Your carrier cannot change the key terms or the price of a fixed-term contract without your express consent. "We're updating your plan" notices that raise your price mid-contract are generally not allowed.

7. The Critical Information Summary

You're entitled to a clear, two-page Critical Information Summary stating your price, term, data limits, and key terms. If your bill doesn't match it, you have a strong complaint.

8. A trial period

You can cancel a new contract within a trial period (typically 15 days, longer for customers with disabilities) at no cost, as long as usage is within set limits.
