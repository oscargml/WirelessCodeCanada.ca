How to Switch Phone Providers — Penalty-Free

Since October 30, 2025, carriers can no longer charge you to leave. That removes the single biggest barrier to switching. Here's how to do it cleanly and keep your number.

Good news: No switching fees, no cancellation fees. If your old carrier tries to bill one, it's recoverable — generate a refund letter.

Step 1 — Check what (if anything) you still owe

You can't be charged a fee to leave, but you may still owe the balance of a device financing agreement (the phone itself). That's separate from a cancellation penalty and is still valid. Ask for your device balance in writing.

Step 2 — Pick your new plan before you cancel

Don't cancel first. Choose your new provider and plan, then port — your number moves automatically and service is continuous.

Step 3 — Port your number (don't cancel manually)

When you sign up with the new carrier, give them your current number and account details. They handle the transfer, which automatically closes your old line. Porting usually takes a few hours.

Step 4 — Confirm the old account is closed at $0

Check your final bill. Watch for: a wrongful cancellation fee (banned), service charges past your port date, or an unreturned-device charge if you kept your phone. Dispute anything that doesn't belong.

Step 5 — Unlock your phone if you're keeping it

All phones must be unlocked free on request. Get it unlocked before or right after switching so it works on the new network.
